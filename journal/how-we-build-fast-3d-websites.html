How we build 3D websites that stay fast

Immersive 3D is the quickest way for a brand to feel premium online, and the quickest way to lose visitors if it is built carelessly. Here is the checklist we follow on every project.

1. Set a performance budget before designing

We decide the limits first: how many megabytes the page may download, how long before something is visible, and the frame rate we expect on a mid-range phone. Every design decision is then checked against that budget, instead of discovering problems at the end.

2. Compress every model and texture

Raw 3D files are enormous. We run models through mesh compression and convert textures to modern formats like WebP at the size they will actually appear. On a recent build, four product models went from 28 MB to 2.4 MB with no visible loss of quality.

3. Only load 3D where it is used

The 3D engine is loaded on demand, only on pages that need it. Case studies, service pages and the journal load as plain, fast HTML, which also keeps them easy for search engines to read.

4. Design a phone version on purpose

Phones get fewer polygons, smaller textures, a lower pixel ratio and simpler post-processing. We also fix the canvas size so it does not jump when the mobile address bar moves, which is one of the most common causes of glitchy 3D on phones.

5. Respect people who prefer less motion

If someone has turned on reduced motion in their device settings, we pause animation and smooth scrolling. It is an accessibility requirement and a sign of care.

6. Keep the words

Every headline, service description and case study is real HTML text. The 3D layer adds atmosphere; the words do the explaining, for people and for Google.
